# Playing Throwabone

Drag sideways on the court or the throw pad to aim. Pull down for power, then release. The dashed orange arc estimates flight under gravity up to the first ground contact. The orange crosshair marks the aim on the target line; the cyan trail records the actual throw. It does not predict subsequent bounces or slide. Small adjustment buttons and a Throw bone button offer an alternative to dragging. Focus the court or pad for arrow-key adjustments and Space to throw; R resets from the court.

Both control modes start in First person: eye height behind the home row, facing the targets across the 10 m court. Enable Follow beside the view buttons to track the bone through its arc, bounces and roll. The camera eases back to the selected view when the throw settles or Follow is switched off. Follow starts off and works with slow motion. The home row is a visual reference; the far row remains the scored, dynamic practice targets. The target close-up stays visible. Target looks toward the row, Side looks along it from a low angle to show impacts and tipping, and Above looks down on the court. Portrait layouts widen the field of view to keep the action in frame.

Switch between fine gravel, packed dirt, short grass and indoor wood. Changing surface resets the targets. Use Target, Above and slow motion to inspect collisions. After a throw settles, the journal records the actual path, first ground contact, release settings and newly fallen targets. Select a throw to compare it against recent throws on the same surface. The arrow in the top-down diagram overlays that recorded path on the court.

This is unlimited practice with the guard-first rule, not a full match. A soldier that falls before both guards is highlighted amber and reset after the throw settles, even if the last guard falls later in that throw. The first early knockdown places it one bone-width inside the nearest standing guard, the second outside, and the third or later upright where it landed with its yaw preserved. Overflow goes behind the reset row. Early knockdowns do not count toward the score. Alternating ends and the hammer are not implemented. Fallen targets and thrown bones remain physical obstacles. The oldest thrown bone is removed before a thirteenth thrower is added to the range. Reset clears the court but preserves the journal. A target that returns upright is counted as standing again.

Known issue: first-landing measurements and rapid repeat throws have confirmed defects. See [release readiness](release.md) before treating the journal as reliable coaching.
