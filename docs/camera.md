# Camera controls and setup

Keyboard / touch mode uses the drag pad and arrow keys. Slide, Tumble and Lob presets combine angle and spin into one throw-style choice. Fine-tune arc and spin remains available as an optional disclosure.

Selecting Camera mode opens an Enable camera dialog. Its button requests video directly from the click; microphone access is never requested. Accept the browser prompt if it appears. If access is denied, the dialog shows the current site address, Chrome site-permission and macOS camera-permission instructions, and a Try again button. Localhost and 127.0.0.1 have separate permissions. Missing or busy cameras receive their own guidance. Closing the dialog returns to keyboard mode and stops any camera granted after closing. After switching tabs, Restart camera starts a fresh session. The app cannot change a saved browser or system permission itself. See [Chrome camera help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/2693767) and [macOS camera permissions](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/control-access-to-the-camera-mchlf6d108da/mac).

Place a camera level at waist-to-chest height on a stable counter or stand, then step back to fit both shoulders, hips and the throwing arm. The live checklist and framing box indicate whether the pose landmarks support tracking. Camera height itself is not measurable from this view; the placement advice is manual setup guidance.

From that position, hold your selected throwing hand above your shoulder for one second to arm a throw. A large on-field prompt shows the hold progress and turns green when armed. Lower the hand beside your hip and pause briefly to set a neutral position. Move it left or right to steer the orange crosshair, then swing underhand. Aim uses wrist position relative to the shoulders, scaled to shoulder width and smoothed over time. The current aim is frozen as the starting point for each gesture so live preview updates cannot accumulate their own offset. The aim meter and spin readout show what will be applied at release. Each throw needs a new raise-and-hold gesture after the court settles; a raised follow-through cannot arm another throw. Tracking loss, a busy court or cancellation clears arming. The Arm one throw button remains available as an alternative.

The MediaPipe lite pose model runs in a dedicated worker using local SDK and model assets loaded only for camera mode. The camera requests up to 30 fps; inference is capped at about 11 fps while preparing and 22 fps while armed, with one frame in flight at a time. Actual rates depend on the device and enabled models. An explicitly armed underhand swing maps normalized upward wrist speed to game power and lateral wrist movement to aim. These are game-control estimates, not measurements of physical release velocity or a real bone's spin. Loft and the fallback spin come from the selected throw style. Low-confidence or lost tracking cancels the gesture. One arming allows one release. Use an empty hand. Closing camera mode or hiding the tab stops all camera tracks and terminates the worker. Video and landmarks are neither saved nor uploaded.

Hand joints are enabled by default in Camera mode. The Show hand joints switch above the preview can turn them off. MediaPipe Hand Landmarker runs in the same worker and draws all 21 landmarks per detected hand in cyan. The preview reports loading, unavailable, looking for hands or the visible joint count. Drawing joints does not require a full body pose or a valid motion reading, so close-up hands remain visible. Motion estimates use stricter checks: the hand must match the selected pose wrist by image position, and tiny, ambiguous or lost hands clear the reading. [Hand Landmarker documentation](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker/web_js).

Wrist controls spin is on by default. A full palm orientation is built from the wrist and knuckles, and changes in orientation estimate signed angular velocity on three axes. Three successive intervals are filtered with a component-wise median; small motion below 0.1 rps is suppressed, tracking gaps reset the estimate, and total spin is limited to 5 rps. This detects twists about the palm normal as well as tilting. The resulting vector is mapped into the game coordinates and passed to Rapier at release. Throw history saves both the vector and its source. A missing estimate or disabling wrist spin uses the selected style's end-over-end spin. Switching back to keyboard/touch restores those manual settings. Finger opening remains a separate readout and does not itself create spin or trigger release. The mapping still needs comparison with recorded throws; the bone's actual angular velocity after leaving the fingers is not directly measurable from an empty-hand gesture. Spin affects orientation and collisions; it does not add an artificial sideways curve to flight.

Camera mode needs a secure browser context (HTTPS or localhost), camera permission and browser support for workers, ImageBitmap and the model runtime. The synthetic landmark tests pass, but a live webcam and physical mobile device have not yet been tested. Camera calibration thresholds are provisional. [MediaPipe pose documentation](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/web_js) describes the underlying landmark detector.

The camera failure-recovery and live-device work is tracked in [release readiness](release.md). For message flow and ownership, see [architecture](architecture.md).
